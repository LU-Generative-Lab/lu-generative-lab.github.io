I view mathematics through the lens of natural philosophy, and that perspective guides my interests: how can we come to see why things are the way they are, and not just verify that they are? My research is in discrete geometry and geometric optimization: how densely cylinders, pentagons, or spheres can be packed, how evenly points can be spread over a sphere, why some knotted rope will not pull apart. I like problems that are approachable by synthetic or analytic means, and those where brute force computation is becoming tractable. I am also interested in building physical models of some of these things.
