Ideas often show up well before they are ready to be shared, and there is a long tradition of writing them down with a date attached: Galileo, Hooke, and Huygens used anagrams, and the Paris Académie still keeps sealed envelopes (Doeblin's was opened in 2000, sixty years after he wrote it). Under Seal is a modern version. The note stays private, its hash gets a public timestamp, and later the note can be opened and read exactly as it was written. Sealing a note is one of the examples that [Muscle Memory](/projects/muscle-memory/) gives of a routine worth writing down as a skill. [Paper Trail](/projects/paper-trail/) can record a sealed note as well: the hash goes into its ledger, with the proof kept beside it, so that private work still has a dated trace.
